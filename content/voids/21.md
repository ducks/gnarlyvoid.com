The wing is carved. That changes everything.

Most tufted work earns its depth through pile alone; here the drake's wing
rides over the body in sculpted relief, so the bird actually stands off the
wall. Cream shoulder, brown coverts, the blue speculum flashing between black
and white bars - the one part of a mallard that looks designed gets the most
dimensional treatment.

Behind him the scene goes cubist: a red sun scored with black rules, gray-green
bands of horizon, a ground plane shattered into blue and gray triangles. The
bird is all field-guide fidelity - bottle-green head, white collar, chestnut
breast, orange feet - standing in a landscape that gave up on realism entirely.
He doesn't seem to mind.

A sculpted cream border traces the whole silhouette and squares it off, part
frame, part halo, keeping the composition one object instead of a bird pasted
on a backdrop.

Drake holds the tension between taxidermy and toy - anatomically earnest,
structurally playful, a specimen mounted in a world made of shapes.

He's a piece in the [Birdhaus](https://birdhaus.art) series - forms over
feathers - the red sun and its black bars carried from screen into pile.
