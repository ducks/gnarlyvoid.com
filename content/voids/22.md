The oldest random number generator, rebuilt as an object with opinions.

A die at many times scale, tufted in deep cream pile with black pips
over a dense foam core. Six up top, five on the face. The form could
not be simpler - a cube and some circles - and that's what makes it
work: dice are pure geometry with rules attached, and scaling one up in
fiber keeps the geometry while quietly retiring the rules.

The piece is a nod to Angine de Poitrine, a band I love whose whole
visual world runs on dice and black-and-white. This is that aesthetic
made physical: monochrome, blunt, a little absurd, sitting on the couch
like it owns the place.

The core is the honest part. Dense foam, not stuffing - press into the
pile and the surface gives, but the cube pushes back and holds its
corners. A cube asks for edges, pile wants to round everything off, and
the foam is what lets both win: crisp enough to read as a die across
the room, soft enough that reading it up close means touching it.

Every die promises a fair result. This one has never once been rolled.
