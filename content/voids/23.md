Two decks for a show, tufted instead of painted.

These went into a group skateboard deck show at [ADX
Portland](https://artdesignxchange.com), the maker studio I'm a member
of.
The brief is the deck shape - that long rounded pill everyone knows -
and what you do inside it is yours. I did what I always do: I reached
for yarn instead of paint.

Deck 001, on the left, is monochrome and hard-edged - cream, grays, and
black cut into arcs, blocks, and angular shards, a little cubist, a
little Bauhaus. It's the more structural of the two: the composition
does the work, and the palette stays out of its way.

Deck 002 is the opposite instinct. Vertical bands of cream, rust,
orange, gold, and brown running the length of the deck, a warm sunset
laid down in stripes. No drawing, just color doing all the talking -
the earth-tone cousin of the traffic-light and Birdhaus pieces.

Same shape, two grammars: one that composes and one that glows. Hung
side by side on the same wall, they read as a conversation - which is
the fun of making two for one show instead of one.

A skateboard is built to be ridden and scuffed. These are built to be
touched and hung. Same silhouette, opposite fate.
