Loaded

July 26, 2026

#tufting #fiber #geometric

The oldest random number generator, rebuilt as an object with opinions.

A die at many times scale, tufted in deep cream pile with black pips over a dense foam core. Six up top, five on the face. The form could not be simpler - a cube and some circles - and that's what makes it work: dice are pure geometry with rules attached, and scaling one up in fiber keeps the geometry, the rules, and the game.

The piece is a nod to Angine de Poitrine, a band I love whose whole visual world runs on dice and black-and-white. This is that aesthetic made physical: monochrome, blunt, a little absurd, sitting on the couch like it owns the place.

The core is the honest part. Dense foam, not stuffing - press into the pile and the surface gives, but the cube pushes back and holds its corners. A cube asks for edges, pile wants to round everything off, and the foam is what lets both win: crisp enough to read as a die across the room, soft enough that reading it up close means touching it.

And it rolls. Underhand, across the floor, tumbling in slow motion and landing with a soft thud and a verdict. Every die promises a fair result; this one just takes its time delivering.
